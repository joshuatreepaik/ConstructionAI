One-Shot Pipeline v2

SHEET PIPE-01
ConstructionAI · one-shot symbol detection

The system answers one question — “how many X are on this drawing, and how sure are you?” — by splitting it into three smaller questions that fail in different ways, and giving each a stage that owns it. Data flows top to bottom; the policy file and the calibration loop stand beside the pipeline, not inside it.

Fig. 1 — The pipeline
Why three stages own three different mistakes

One wrong count, three different diseases

When the system says “receptacle here,” three unrelated things could be wrong. Each needs different information to fix, so each gets a different owner — this is the idea the whole architecture hangs on.

Geometric

“No symbol is there at all.”

A hatch pattern or chair arc mimicked the shape. Fixed by better geometry: rarity-weighted votes, log-odds against clutter.

Stages 1 + 3

Semantic

“It’s a symbol, but the wrong type.”

GFCI and duplex differ by a printed “G”, not by geometry. Fixed by reading the text tag sitting on the symbol.

Stage 4 · semantic

Scope

“Real symbol — but it shouldn’t count.”

The legend’s own example, the key-plan inset, the compass rose. Fixed by knowing which region of the sheet you’re on.

Data contract

What flows between stages

Sheet→ Candidate→ Candidate + Evidence→ Detection→ Report

Every proposer emits the same Candidate; every candidate gets an Evidence score on the same log-odds scale; every survivor becomes a Detection carrying its class, subclass, scope and decision — with the reason attached when rejected. The confidence slider is just a moving line through the stored evidence scores.

Proof the stages earn their keep

Back-to-back door fix: 26 → 35 doors per sheet
Cross-check: T5 = T8 = 33 standard doors, E4 agrees

The door fix is the cautionary tale: nine doors per sheet were silently merged away by a duplicate rule, invisible in the total, caught only when a human looked at two specific doors — the argument for the review queue and for ground-truth evaluation, in one bug.
